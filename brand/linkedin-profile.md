## 1. Name, and why it is not "ArixPro"

```
First name:  Rao
Last name:   Rizwan
```

LinkedIn requires the name fields to hold a real person's name. It strips
company names, job titles, emoji and keywords out of them, and profiles that
keep putting them back get restricted. So no "ArixPro" in the surname, and no
"Rao Rizwan | Coding Instructor".

You lose nothing by this. The two fields that actually carry brand and keywords
are the headline and the custom URL, and both stay:

- **Custom URL stays `linkedin.com/in/arixpro`.** A vanity URL does not have to
  match your name, and keeping the brand word in the URL is worth more for
  search than putting it in the surname would have been.
- **The headline below leads with ArixPro.**

## 2. Headline

The most important field for search. LinkedIn indexes it, and it follows you
into every feed, comment and search result. 220 character limit; this is 193.

```
Founder at ArixPro | I teach Python, web development, backend and AI to beginners in Pakistan | Live one on one and small batches, Lahore and online | Concepts in Urdu, technical terms in English
```

Every term someone would search for appears once: Python, web development,
backend, AI, Lahore, Pakistan, online. The Urdu line is the differentiator, so
it earns its place in the headline rather than being buried in the About.

## 3. Profile photo

This one has to be you. A real face on a founder profile outperforms a logo,
and a logo on a `/in/` profile is one of the signals LinkedIn uses to spot a
company posing as a person.

What works: shoulders up, your face filling most of the frame, even light on
your face, uncluttered background. Square, at least 400 x 400. It is masked to
a circle and often rendered at 48px, so anything full-body disappears.

The mark stays the profile picture everywhere it belongs. `brand/avatar/` still
has it for GitHub, Instagram, Facebook and WhatsApp.

## 10. What actually moves this in search

Roughly in order of effect, and almost none of it is on the profile itself:

1. **Post consistently.** LinkedIn ranks profiles that generate engagement. Two
   or three posts a week beats a perfect profile that never posts.
2. **Comment on other people's posts** in Pakistani tech and student
   communities. On a small account, comments reach more new people than posts.
3. **Connections.** Search results are weighted by degree of connection.
   Connect with students, university societies and local developers.
4. **Recreate the Company Page.** Google reads a Company Page as the
   organisation's identity through the `sameAs` block on arixpro.com. A personal
   profile does not carry that signal, and a `/in/` profile run as a company is
   the setup LinkedIn restricts.
5. **The website already links here.** Footer and structured data both point at
   this profile.
